Second-home watch

If you close up a Cape house for the winter, you want to know about a burst pipe in January, not in April. I set up sensors and a small box that reports to you.

What gets installed

  • Leak sensors in three places as standard: the water heater, the kitchen or a bath, and the basement sump or lowest floor. More can be added.
  • Temperature sensors: one in the coldest room, one near a water line or the heating system, off exterior walls.
  • Cameras, if you want them. Audio is off, they cover entrances and the driveway only, never bedrooms or bathrooms, and they send motion alerts with no recording unless you ask. You tell renters and cleaners in writing.
  • A small hub that collects the readings and sends alerts to your phone and email.

Which of these you need depends on the house. We decide together on a first call, and I send you the equipment list and cost before anything is bought. I walk the house with you at install.

What alerts you get

  • Water detected.
  • Temperature falls below a limit you choose (50 °F unless you pick another).
  • Power or internet goes out at the house.
  • Motion at the camera, if you have one.
  • A monthly check-in message so you know the system is still running.

Leak and cold alerts reach your phone at the same time as mine; a power cut alert follows after about five minutes. Alerts come through a free phone app (ntfy) that I help you set up, and by email. If the internet goes down or the house stops reporting, I get the alert and I tell you. I follow up during weeknight evenings and weekends, by appointment, and I do not watch overnight. I watch the system health and sort out faults in the setup, but I am not a security guard or an alarm company. For an emergency at the house, call 911 and your local keyholder or property manager. It is not fire or carbon monoxide monitoring. It tells you about a leak but does not stop one, and every house needs a named local keyholder.

What it costs

We sign a short written agreement that says what I watch, what I do when an alert comes in, and how either of us can stop.
