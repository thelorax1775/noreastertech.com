Rook by Nor'easter Tech

A small box that sits between your internet and your home network, set up and looked after by me. It blocks known trackers and bad sites, keeps visitors and smart-home gadgets off your main network, and shows you what is connected.

What it does

  • Blocks known ad, tracker and malware sites for every device on the network, including the smart TV that has no ad blocker of its own.
  • Keeps devices from skipping the filter. Many TVs and streaming sticks ask their own DNS server instead of yours. Rook sends those requests to its own filter.
  • Shows every device on your network and tells you when a new one joins.
  • Separate networks for family, guests, smart-home gadgets and cameras, so a visitor's phone or a cheap smart plug cannot reach your laptop.
  • A firewall and intrusion detection that watches for known attack patterns.
  • An optional VPN for chosen devices, through Mullvad. It is off unless you ask for it.
  • Health checks and remote care. I see if Rook or your internet has a problem, and I can fix many things without a visit.
  • A simple web page at rook.local on your home network, in plain words: devices, networks, protection.

What it does not do

  • It does not make you anonymous or untrackable. Websites you log in to, your phone's apps and the companies you buy from still know who you are.
  • It does not stop a device that runs its own VPN, or an app that hides its lookups inside ordinary web traffic.
  • It is not an antivirus, and it cannot promise to stop every attack. It lowers the risk.
  • If you want to keep your internet provider's router, Rook can sit beside it. Then it filters and shows devices, but it cannot separate networks or force devices to use its filter, because that traffic never goes through it. I will tell you which mode fits your house.

Your lookups and browsing history stay on the box in your home. My remote care only sees whether Rook is healthy and summaries of security events, not the sites you visit. I can see device names and counts when I help you.

Who it is for

  • Families with lots of phones, tablets, TVs and smart gadgets.
  • Second-home owners, with the second-home watch included.
  • Small offices of fewer than ten people that want guest Wi-Fi kept apart from the work network.
